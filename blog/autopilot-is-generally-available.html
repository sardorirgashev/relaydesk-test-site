Relay AI Autopilot is now generally available

Autopilot now answers your customers on chat and email from your Knowledge Base, and hands off to your team the moment it should.

By Daniel Reyes · Published November 12, 2025 · 4 min read

Today Relay AI Autopilot is generally available to every Relaydesk workspace on the Growth and Scale plans. After months in beta, Autopilot can now answer your customers directly on live chat and email, using the articles in your Knowledge Base, and hand off to your team the moment it should.

I want to cover what Autopilot does, how the handoff works, exactly how we count an “AI resolution”, and what we learned from the teams who used it during the beta.

What Autopilot does

Relay AI has two modes. Copilot, which launched in 2024, works alongside your agents: it drafts replies for them to review and summarizes long threads. Autopilot goes a step further and replies to customers on its own.

When a customer starts a chat or sends an email, Autopilot reads the message, searches your published Knowledge Base articles for the answer, and writes a reply in your brand's voice. It cites only what is in your help center; internal-only articles are never shown to customers. If the customer is satisfied, the conversation closes. If not, a human takes over.

A few principles guided how we built it:

  • Your knowledge, not the internet's. Autopilot answers from your Knowledge Base. If the answer isn't there, it doesn't guess.
  • Your data stays yours. Relay AI is never trained on customer data across workspaces. Nothing one business writes improves answers for another.
  • Humans are one step away. Customers can always reach a person, and they never have to fight a bot to get there.

How handoff works

Autopilot hands a conversation to a human in two situations: when its confidence in an answer is low, and when the customer asks for a person. Phrases like “talk to someone” or “this isn't helping” are enough.

When that happens, the conversation drops into your Shared Inbox and follows your normal assignment rules, whether that is round-robin, load-balanced or skills-based. The agent who picks it up sees the full transcript plus a Copilot summary at the top, so they never have to ask the customer to repeat themselves. Your SLA policies apply from the moment of handoff.

During the beta, the single most common reason for a low-confidence handoff was simple: the answer wasn't written down anywhere. That is why Knowledge Base search analytics, especially the “searches with no results” report, is the best companion to Autopilot. Fill the gaps it shows you and your resolution rate goes up.

What counts as an AI resolution

We bill Autopilot per resolution, so we wanted the definition to be strict and easy to check. An AI resolution is a conversation that Autopilot closes with no human reply in the following 72 hours.

That 72-hour window matters. If a customer gets an answer from Autopilot, comes back the next day and needs an agent, the conversation does not count as an AI resolution. We only count conversations where Autopilot genuinely finished the job. Handoffs are never billed.

Growth includes 500 AI resolutions per month and Scale includes 2,000. Beyond that, each extra resolution is $0.60. New workspaces get 50 AI resolutions as part of the 14-day free trial, so you can try Autopilot on real conversations before you commit. Full plan details are on the pricing page.

What we learned in the beta

Over the beta, 312 workspaces turned on Autopilot for at least one channel. Across all of them, Autopilot produced an AI resolution on 31% of the conversations it handled. Teams with well-maintained help centers did much better than that. Three of them agreed to share their numbers.

Fernhill, a Shopify store with a small team of five agents, saw the biggest gains. Most of its questions are about order status, returns and sizing, which its Knowledge Base covers in detail. “Our agents stopped answering ‘where's my order’ forty times a day,” their support lead told us. “Now they spend that time on the customers who actually need a person.”

Harbor & Vine had a lower resolution rate, and that was by design. They configured Autopilot to hand off anything that mentioned symptoms or treatment, and use it only for appointment logistics, insurance questions and office hours. We think that is exactly the right way to use it.

We also changed several things because of beta feedback. Handoff summaries are now shorter and lead with what the customer wants. Autopilot replies are shorter by default. And admins can see, conversation by conversation, which article Autopilot used for each answer.

Getting started

If you are on Growth or Scale, you can turn on Autopilot today:

  1. Review your Knowledge Base. Make sure your top questions have clear, published articles.
  2. Turn on Autopilot for live chat, email or both, and decide how it should introduce itself.
  3. Test it with real questions from your recent tickets before you go live.
  4. Watch the “searches with no results” report and article feedback for the first few weeks, and fill the gaps.

The full setup guide is in Set up Relay AI, and you can read more about both modes on the Relay AI page. Chat and email are just the start; we are already working on bringing Autopilot to more channels.
