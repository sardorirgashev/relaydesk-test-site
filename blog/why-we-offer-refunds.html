Why we offer refunds

Our co-founder and CEO on why we make it easy to get your money back, and what refund requests have taught us.

By Maya Okafor · Published April 22, 2024 · 5 min read

Every few months someone on our team asks a fair question: why do we make it so easy to get your money back? Refunds cost us revenue, they take time from our billing team, and a handful of people will always try to game them. A lot of software companies simply say “all sales are final” and move on.

We have offered refunds since the week Daniel and I started charging for Relaydesk, back in a small office in Portland in 2019. I want to explain why, what the policy actually is, and why I think it makes us a better company rather than a more generous one.

The policy, in one sentence

Here it is, plainly: you can get a full refund within 14 days of your first payment. That applies whether you pay monthly or annually and whichever plan you choose. You don't need to give a reason, fill in a survey or talk to a retention specialist. Email billing@relaydesk.example from your billing contact address, tell us your workspace name, and we will process it.

That's it. We don't refund partial months after that window, and usage-based charges such as SMS messages that have already been delivered aren't refundable, because those costs have already been paid to the carriers on your behalf. But for the subscription itself, the first 14 days after you pay are yours to decide.

A free trial isn't the same as using the product for real

We already have a 14-day free trial with no credit card required, so people sometimes ask why we need a refund window on top of it. The honest answer is that a trial and real usage are different things.

During a trial, most teams connect one email address, send a few test tickets to themselves and click around. That tells you whether the interface feels right. It does not tell you whether Relaydesk survives a Monday morning after a product launch, when 300 customers write in at once and your newest agent is on their second day. You only find that out after you have moved your whole team over, connected every channel and switched off your old tool. By then you have paid.

A refund window is our way of saying: we know the real test happens after the trial, and we will stand behind the product through it.

Refunds keep us honest

When you can't get your money back, a company's incentive is to get you to pay and then stop paying attention. When you can, the incentive flips. Every refund request is a signal that something didn't work, and it arrives quickly enough for us to do something about it.

We read every refund request. Priya Natarajan, who leads our Customer Experience team, reviews the reasons each week with product and engineering. Some of the most useful changes we have made came straight from them:

  • Several early refunds came from teams whose help address forwarded through a mail provider that rewrote headers. We rebuilt our email parsing so threads stay together no matter how mail is forwarded.
  • A cluster of requests from shops with seasonal staff told us that seat billing felt punishing. That is why we introduced free light seats for people who only need to read tickets and leave internal notes.
  • Agencies told us they couldn't work out which inbox a ticket came from. Views and tags got a lot better because of them.

None of those customers owed us an explanation. Most gave one anyway, because we made leaving easy and they were glad to help the next team avoid the same problem.

“Won't people abuse it?”

Some will. In practice it is a very small number, and the cost is far lower than people expect. A helpdesk is not a product you can quietly use for two weeks and walk away with; you have to move your customers' conversations into it. People who do that work are usually looking for a home, not a free ride.

The bigger risk is the opposite one. If we make refunds hard, we lose the trust of the 99% of customers who would never abuse them, just to stop the 1% who might. That is a bad trade for a company whose whole product is about treating customers well.

What we ask in return

We do ask for two things. First, if you are going to request a refund, please tell us why, even in one line. It isn't a condition, but it is the most valuable feedback we get. Second, export anything you want to keep before you close your workspace. Admins can download a full export in CSV or JSON, and once a workspace is deleted we really do delete the data.

We also ask for a little patience on timing. Once we approve a refund, it goes back to the original card, and banks usually take a few business days to show it.

Support people built this policy

Daniel and I both spent years answering customer emails before we started Relaydesk. I ran support at a small outdoor gear retailer, and the thing I hated most was telling a frustrated customer that the policy said no. Policies like that don't protect a business. They just move the cost from the balance sheet to the support inbox, where an agent has to absorb the anger.

When we wrote our own policy, we wrote the one we wished we could have offered. If a business is going to trust us with every conversation it has with its customers, the least we can do is make it easy to change its mind.

If you are thinking about Relaydesk, start with the free trial. If you move your team over and it isn't right, you have 14 days after your first payment to tell us, and we'll give you your money back. And if you have questions before then, our team is one email away at support@relaydesk.example, or you can find us on the contact page.

— Maya Okafor, co-founder and CEO
