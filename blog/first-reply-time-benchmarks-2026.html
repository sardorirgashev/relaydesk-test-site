First reply time benchmarks for 2026

Median first reply times by channel and industry from six months of anonymized Relaydesk data, and what the fastest teams do differently.

By Priya Natarajan · Published September 3, 2026 · 4 min read

How fast should you reply to a customer? It is one of the questions our Customer Experience team hears most. The honest answer is “it depends on the channel and on your customers,” which is true but not very useful. So we looked at the data.

This report covers first reply times across Relaydesk workspaces from January 1 to June 30, 2026. It is built from anonymized, aggregate data only: we never look at the content of conversations for this work, and no individual workspace can be identified from the results.

How we measured

First reply time is the time from a customer's first message in a new conversation to the first public reply from a human agent. We used these rules:

  • The sample covers 4,120 workspaces that had at least 100 conversations in the period, about 41 million conversations in total.
  • Times are measured in calendar time, not business hours, so a message sent on Saturday and answered on Monday counts the full weekend. Your own SLA reports may look better if you attach a business hours schedule.
  • Conversations that Relay AI Autopilot resolved, and spam, are excluded. If Autopilot handed a conversation to a human, we measured the time to that human's first reply.
  • We report the median rather than the average, because a few tickets that wait for days would otherwise distort the picture.

The gap between channels is huge, and it mirrors what customers expect. Nobody waits four hours in a chat window. Many people are happy with a same-morning answer by email. Messaging channels sit in the middle, and they are where we see the most variation: the fastest teams treat WhatsApp like chat, while slower ones treat it like email.

Ecommerce teams are fastest overall because so much of their volume arrives through chat and messaging. Software companies have the quickest email replies, likely because many run support around the clock for customers in several time zones. Education and nonprofit teams are slowest, which matches what we hear: they often run support with part-time staff or volunteers alongside other jobs.

What the fastest teams do differently

We compared the fastest quarter of workspaces in each industry with the rest. Five habits stood out.

1. They set SLA policies and attach business hours

Workspaces with at least one active SLA policy had a median email first reply time 38% lower than those without. A target makes the queue visible: agents can see which tickets are close to breaching, and managers can see where the backlog is building. Attaching a business hours schedule keeps the targets fair. See Create SLA policies.

2. They route automatically

Teams using load-balanced or skills-based assignment replied faster than teams that let agents pick tickets from a shared view. Automatic routing removes the moment where a ticket sits unclaimed because everyone assumes someone else has it. Start with Set up assignment rules.

3. They keep a short list of great macros

The fastest teams didn't have more macros; they had better ones. A typical fast workspace used 20 to 40 macros regularly, kept them up to date and used variables to personalize them. Read Use macros for tips.

4. They let Relay AI take the first pass

Workspaces using Copilot to draft replies had a 27% lower median email first reply time than similar workspaces that didn't. And where Autopilot is turned on for chat and email, the conversations it resolves never reach the queue at all, which frees agents for the ones that need a person. See Relay AI.

5. They don't let chat go dark

Fast teams switch Live Chat to offline mode when no one is available, so chats become email tickets instead of sitting unanswered in a widget. A missed chat is far worse for customers than an honest “we'll email you back.”

Speed isn't everything

A fast first reply that doesn't help is worse than a slightly slower one that does. Across the sample, CSAT scores rose with faster first replies up to a point, then leveled off: on email, customers who got a reply in under an hour rated their experience about the same as those who got one in under ten minutes. Aim for a target your team can meet consistently, then put the extra effort into resolving the issue in one reply.
