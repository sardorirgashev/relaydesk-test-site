Connect your support email

Forward your existing support address to Relaydesk and every email becomes a ticket in the Shared Inbox. Add two DNS records and your replies go out from your own domain.

Last updated May 19, 2026 · 4 min read

Connecting email takes two parts. Receiving: you forward mail from your support mailbox to Relaydesk. Sending: you add DNS records so Relaydesk can send replies as your domain. You can do the first part in five minutes and the second whenever your DNS admin is available, but don't reply to customers from Relaydesk until sending is verified, or replies may land in spam.

Each support address you connect counts as one email inbox. Starter includes 2 inboxes, Growth 10 and Scale unlimited.

Find your forwarding address

Go to Settings → Channels → Email and click Add inbox. Enter the address your customers write to, for example support@northwind.example. Relaydesk shows your forwarding address, which always has this form:

support-<workspace>@inbound.relaydesk.example

For a workspace with the handle northwind, that's support-northwind@inbound.relaydesk.example. Copy it; you'll paste it into your mail provider next.

Forward from Gmail or Google Workspace

  1. Sign in to the support mailbox in Gmail and open Settings → See all settings → Forwarding and POP/IMAP.
  2. Click Add a forwarding address and paste your Relaydesk forwarding address.
  3. Gmail sends a confirmation email to that address. It appears in Relaydesk as a ticket from Gmail Team within a minute. Open it and click the confirmation link, or copy the code into Gmail.
  4. Back in Gmail, choose Forward a copy of incoming mail to your Relaydesk address and pick keep Gmail's copy in the Inbox or archive Gmail's copy.
  5. Click Save changes.
Google Workspace admins: if the forwarding option is greyed out, automatic forwarding is disabled for your organization. Turn it on in the Google Admin console under Apps → Google Workspace → Gmail → End user access → Automatic forwarding.

Forward from Microsoft 365

  1. In Outlook on the web, open the support mailbox and go to Settings → Mail → Forwarding.
  2. Turn on Enable forwarding and paste your Relaydesk forwarding address.
  3. Select Keep a copy of forwarded messages if you want mail to stay in Outlook too.
  4. Click Save.

For a shared mailbox, an Exchange admin can set forwarding instead in the Microsoft 365 admin center: open the mailbox under Teams & groups → Shared mailboxes, choose Email forwarding and enter the Relaydesk address.

Forwarding blocked? Microsoft 365 blocks automatic external forwarding by default. If test emails never reach Relaydesk, ask your admin to allow automatic forwarding for the support mailbox in the outbound spam filter policy in Microsoft Defender.

Send from your own domain

To send replies as support@northwind.example, Relaydesk needs permission to send mail for your domain. You grant it with SPF and DKIM DNS records. DMARC is not required, but we strongly recommend it, because many mailbox providers now treat mail without DMARC as suspicious.

Relaydesk shows the exact records for your domain under Settings → Channels → Email → your inbox → Sending domain. They look like this:

PurposeTypeHost / nameValue
SPFTXTnorthwind.examplev=spf1 include:spf.relaydesk.example ~all
DKIM (key 1)CNAMErd1._domainkey.northwind.examplerd1.dkim.relaydesk.example
DKIM (key 2)CNAMErd2._domainkey.northwind.examplerd2.dkim.relaydesk.example
DMARC (recommended)TXT_dmarc.northwind.examplev=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@northwind.example
  • Already have an SPF record? Don't add a second one. A domain can have only one SPF record, so add include:spf.relaydesk.example to your existing record, before the ~all or -all.
  • DMARC policy. Start with p=none to collect reports without affecting delivery. Once reports show your mail passing, move to p=quarantine or p=reject.

Verify the connection

  1. After adding the records, click Verify DNS on the Sending domain page. Each record shows a green check when it's found. DNS changes can take up to 48 hours to spread, though most appear within an hour.
  2. Click Send test email. Relaydesk sends a message to your support address, which is forwarded back and appears as a ticket. That proves receiving works.
  3. Reply to the test ticket and check the reply in a personal mailbox. It should come from your support address with no "via" or "on behalf of" label.

Attachments

Attachments can be up to 25 MB per file, both for incoming email and for replies your agents send. If a customer sends a larger file, the ticket is still created, but the oversized attachment is left out and an internal note tells the agent so they can ask for a link instead.

Troubleshooting

Emails aren't turning into tickets

Send a message from a personal account to your support address. If it doesn't arrive in Relaydesk within a few minutes, forwarding isn't active. Check the provider steps above, including the Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 admin settings that block forwarding.

Replies go to spam

Check that SPF and DKIM both show as verified. If you have more than one SPF record, merge them into one. Add a DMARC record if you don't have one.

Tickets are created twice

You probably have forwarding set up twice, for example a mailbox rule and a mailbox-level forward. Remove one.

Auto-replies are creating loops

Relaydesk ignores messages marked as automatic replies, but some systems don't mark them. Add a trigger that closes tickets from the looping address without replying, and turn off your mail provider's own auto-reply on the support mailbox; Relaydesk sends its own acknowledgment.

Verify DNS still fails after 48 hours

Check for typos and make sure your DNS host didn't append your domain twice, for example rd1._domainkey.northwind.example.northwind.example. If it still fails, email support@relaydesk.example with a screenshot of your DNS records.
