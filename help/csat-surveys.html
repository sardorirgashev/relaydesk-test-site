Send CSAT surveys

A CSAT survey asks the customer one question after their ticket is solved: how satisfied were you? This article covers when surveys are sent, what customers see, which tickets to leave out, and how to read the results.

Last updated June 3, 2026 · 3 min read

How CSAT surveys work

When a ticket is set to Solved, Relaydesk waits for the configured delay and then sends the customer a short survey. The default delay is 1 hour. You can set it anywhere from 0 to 72 hours. If the customer replies during the delay, the ticket reopens and the survey is canceled. It is scheduled again the next time the ticket is solved.

Each ticket receives at most one survey. Once a customer has rated a ticket, solving it again later does not send a second survey, though the customer can change their rating from the original link.

Tip: A short delay gives better response rates for chat, while the customer is still around. For email-heavy teams, many customers use the 1-hour default so the survey does not land in the same minute as the final reply.

What the customer sees

The survey uses a 5-point scale:

RatingLabelCounts as satisfied?
5Very satisfiedYes
4SatisfiedYes
3NeutralNo
2DissatisfiedNo
1Very dissatisfiedNo

The rating is recorded as soon as the customer clicks or taps. They are then offered an optional follow-up comment box. You can edit the question shown above the box, and show a different one for low ratings (1–3), for example "What could we have done better?". The survey question, labels, and comment prompt are translated automatically into the 32 languages supported by the Live Chat widget, and you can override any translation.

Channels

How the survey is delivered depends on where the conversation happened:

  • Email: a short email with five rating buttons. Clicking a button records the rating and opens the comment page.
  • Live Chat: if the customer still has the widget open, the rating appears inside the chat. Otherwise it is sent by email, provided the pre-chat form or offline mode collected an address.
  • SMS and WhatsApp: a message with a short survey link. Available on Growth and Scale, where these channels are included.
  • Facebook and Instagram DMs: surveys are not sent on these channels.

You can turn surveys on or off per channel under Settings → Reporting → CSAT.

Set up CSAT surveys

  1. Go to Settings → Reporting → CSAT and switch on Send satisfaction surveys.
  2. Set the delay after solve (default 1 hour, 0–72 hours).
  3. Choose the channels that should receive surveys.
  4. Edit the question and the follow-up comment prompts, and preview the email and chat versions.
  5. Add exclusions (see below) and click Save.

Excluding tickets

Not every solved ticket should get a survey. Add exclusion rules for:

  • Tags: any ticket with a tag you choose, such as no-csat, internal, or vendor. Agents can add the tag manually, or a macro can add it.
  • Inboxes: for example a partners inbox or an internal IT inbox.
  • Channels: switch off a channel entirely, as described above.
  • AI resolutions: choose whether conversations closed by Relay AI Autopilot get a survey. They are included by default and reported separately.

Tickets marked as spam and tickets merged into another ticket are always excluded.

Note: Exclusions only apply to future surveys. Responses already received stay in your reports.

CSAT reports

Open Reporting → CSAT to see:

  • CSAT score: the percentage of responses rated 4 or 5. For example, 172 ratings of 4 or 5 out of 200 responses is an 86% CSAT score.
  • Response rate: responses divided by surveys sent.
  • Breakdowns by agent, team, channel, inbox, tag, and Autopilot versus human-handled conversations.
  • Comments feed: every follow-up comment with its rating and a link to the ticket. Filter to low ratings to review what went wrong.

Each rating is also shown on the ticket itself, so agents see feedback in context. The macro usage report uses the same data to show average CSAT per macro. CSAT responses are included in a full workspace data export. See Export your data.
