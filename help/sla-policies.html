Create SLA policies

An SLA policy turns your support promises into timers on every ticket. Set targets by priority, measure them in your business hours, and let Relaydesk warn you, or forecast trouble, before a target is missed.

Last updated October 16, 2026 · 4 min read

What an SLA policy measures

Each SLA policy can set up to three targets. You can use all three or only the ones you report on.

  • First reply time: from ticket creation to the first public reply from an agent. Internal notes do not count.
  • Next reply time: from each customer message to the next public reply, for every back-and-forth after the first.
  • Full resolution time: from ticket creation until the ticket is set to Solved. Time the ticket spends in Pending, waiting on the customer, is excluded.

If a solved ticket is reopened by a customer reply, the next reply timer starts again and the resolution timer resumes from where it stopped.

Business hours

Attach a business hours schedule to a policy and Relaydesk measures every target in business hours only. A one-hour target on a ticket that arrives at 5:30pm on Friday, with a schedule of Monday to Friday 9am–6pm, is due at 9:30am on Monday. Each schedule has its own time zone and holiday calendar, and holidays are skipped like weekends. Without a schedule, timers run 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

The number of schedules depends on your plan: 1 on Starter, up to 5 on Growth, and unlimited on Scale. Set them up under Settings → Workflows → Business hours.

Targets by priority

Within one policy, you set a separate target for each ticket priority. Priority is set by agents, by macros, or by your assignment and automation rules. The table below shows example targets that many Relaydesk customers start from. They are a starting point, not defaults: pick numbers your team can meet on a normal week.

PriorityFirst replyNext replyFull resolution
Urgent30 minutes1 hour4 hours
High1 hour2 hours8 hours
Normal4 hours8 hours2 business days
Low8 hours24 hours5 business days
Tip: Leave a target blank to skip it for that priority. Many teams set no full resolution target for Low tickets and track only first reply time.

Which policy applies to a ticket

Each policy has conditions such as inbox, channel, tag, or customer organization. Policies are checked from top to bottom, and the first policy whose conditions match is applied. A ticket has only one active SLA policy at a time. If a ticket's details change, for example a vip tag is added, Relaydesk re-checks the policies and the new targets apply from that moment.

Create a policy

  1. Go to Settings → Workflows → SLA policies and click New policy.
  2. Name the policy and add conditions, for example Inbox is Support and Tag is enterprise.
  3. Choose a business hours schedule, or leave it empty to measure around the clock.
  4. Enter first reply, next reply, and full resolution targets for Urgent, High, Normal, and Low.
  5. Set breach warnings and who receives them, then click Save.

Breach warnings

Every ticket with an active target shows a timer badge in the ticket list and the ticket header. The badge turns amber when a target is close and red once it is breached. By default, a warning is sent when 75% of a target's time has passed. You can change the threshold per policy, anywhere from 50% to 95%.

Warnings go to the assigned agent, or to the team if the ticket is unassigned, as an in-app notification. You can also send them by email or to a Slack channel through the Slack integration. Breaches are recorded on the ticket and appear in the SLA report under Reporting → SLAs, which shows achievement rate by policy, priority, team, and agent.

SLA breach forecasts

SLA breach forecasts arrived in Relaydesk 2026.10 on October 14, 2026. A warning tells you that one ticket is running out of time. A forecast looks across the whole queue and predicts which tickets are likely to breach in the next 4 hours, before any of them turn amber.

The forecast uses the number of open tickets, which agents are Online, each team's recent handling times, and your business hours. Results refresh every 10 minutes and appear in two places:

  • An At risk view in the Shared Inbox, sorted by predicted breach time.
  • A forecast panel on the SLA dashboard showing expected breaches per team over the next 4 hours.

Use forecasts to move agents between queues, raise a ticket's priority, or bring someone back from Away before the breach happens.

Note: Forecasts are estimates. A sudden spike in new tickets can create breaches the forecast did not predict, so keep breach warnings turned on as well.

Plan limits

Starter includes 1 SLA policy, which applies to all tickets that match its conditions. Growth and Scale include unlimited SLA policies. All plans get the three target types, business hours (within the schedule limits above), breach warnings, and breach forecasts. Compare plans on the pricing page.
