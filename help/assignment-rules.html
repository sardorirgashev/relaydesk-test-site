Set up assignment rules

Assignment rules decide who owns a new ticket the moment it lands in the Shared Inbox, so nothing sits unclaimed while your team is busy. This article covers the three routing methods, agent availability statuses, and what happens when someone signs off.

Last updated September 8, 2026 · 4 min read

How assignment works

Every new ticket, whether it arrives by email, SMS, WhatsApp, Facebook or Instagram DM, or Live Chat, passes through your workspace's assignment rules before it appears in anyone's queue. A rule has two parts: conditions (which tickets it applies to, such as inbox, channel, tag, or customer language) and a method (how it picks an agent from a team). If no rule matches, the ticket stays in the Unassigned view until someone claims it.

Admins manage rules under Settings → Workflows → Assignment rules. Agents can always reassign a ticket by hand, and manual assignment always overrides a rule.

The three assignment methods

MethodHow it picks an agentBest for
Round-robinRotates through Online team members in a fixed order, one ticket each.Teams with similar ticket types and equal shifts.
Load-balancedPicks the Online agent with the fewest open tickets; skips anyone at the maximum open tickets per agent.Mixed workloads where some tickets take much longer than others.
Skills-basedLimits the pool to agents who have every skill the rule requires, then uses round-robin or load-balanced among them.Multilingual support, product specialists, VIP queues.

Round-robin

Round-robin is the simplest method. Relaydesk keeps a pointer for each team and hands the next ticket to the next Online agent in the list. Agents who are Away or Offline are skipped, and the pointer moves on. Round-robin does not look at how many tickets an agent already has, so one long-running escalation can leave an agent behind while the rotation continues.

Load-balanced

Load-balanced assignment counts each agent's tickets in the Open status and gives the new ticket to whoever has the fewest. Set a maximum open tickets per agent (default 20, any value from 1 to 200). When every Online agent on the team has reached the cap, new tickets wait in the team's queue and are assigned as soon as someone solves a ticket and drops below the limit. Pending and On-hold tickets do not count toward the cap.

Skills-based

Skills are labels you give agents under Settings → Team → Skills, for example Spanish, Billing, or Shopify orders. A skills-based rule lists the skills a ticket needs, and only agents with every listed skill are eligible. Choose round-robin or load-balanced as the tie-breaker within that smaller pool. If no eligible agent is Online, the rule can either wait for one or fall through to the next rule. Choose this with the If nobody is available setting.

Tip: Pair skills-based rules with language detection. A rule with the condition Customer language is Portuguese and the required skill Portuguese sends those tickets straight to the right people, while everything else falls through to your general round-robin rule.

Agent availability statuses

Each agent sets their status from the avatar menu in the web app or the iOS and Android agent apps. Assignment rules only ever pick agents who are Online.

  • Online: receives new assignments and appears in the team's rotation.
  • Away: keeps every ticket they already own but receives no new ones. Relaydesk switches an agent to Away automatically after 15 minutes without activity in the agent app.
  • Offline: receives no new assignments. Agents become Offline when they choose it, sign out, or their session times out.

Collision detection works the same in every status. If an Away agent opens a ticket someone else is answering, they still see "Ana is replying…" in the composer.

Reassigning tickets when an agent goes offline

Turn on Reassign open tickets when an agent goes Offline to keep customers from waiting on someone who has finished their shift. After a grace period (default 30 minutes, adjustable from 0 to 240 minutes), the agent's tickets in the Open status go back through the assignment rules as if they were new. Pending and On-hold tickets stay with their owner, because the next step is the customer's or a third party's. If the agent comes back Online during the grace period, nothing is reassigned.

Note: Reassignment only moves tickets. Internal notes, @mentions, and the full conversation history stay on the ticket, so the new owner sees everything the previous agent did.

Order of evaluation

Relaydesk evaluates rules in the order they appear on the Assignment rules page, from top to bottom, and stops at the first rule that matches. Put your most specific rules at the top and a broad catch-all at the bottom.

  1. Manual assignment, including assignment by a macro, always wins and skips the rules.
  2. Rules are checked top to bottom. The first rule whose conditions all match assigns the ticket.
  3. Within that rule, only Online agents are considered, then skills are applied, then the method picks one agent.
  4. If the rule finds nobody and is set to fall through, evaluation continues with the next rule.
  5. If nothing matches, the ticket stays in the Unassigned view.

Create an assignment rule

  1. Go to Settings → Workflows → Assignment rules and click New rule.
  2. Name the rule and add conditions, for example Inbox is Billing and Channel is Email.
  3. Choose the team and the method: round-robin, load-balanced, or skills-based. For load-balanced, set the maximum open tickets per agent.
  4. Choose what happens if nobody is available: wait in the team queue or fall through to the next rule.
  5. Click Save, then drag the rule into the right position in the list.

New rules only apply to tickets created after you save. To set up teams and invite agents first, see Invite teammates. For a full workspace walkthrough, see the admin guide.

Can light seats be assigned tickets?

No. Light (collaborator) seats can view tickets and add internal notes but cannot reply to customers, so assignment rules never pick them.
