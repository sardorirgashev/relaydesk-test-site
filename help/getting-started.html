Getting started with Relaydesk: your first-week checklist

Most teams go from sign-up to answering real customers in less than a week. This checklist walks you through the seven things to do first, in the order that saves you the most rework.

Last updated September 22, 2026 · 4 min read

This guide is for the person setting up Relaydesk for the first time, usually the workspace Owner. You don't need to finish everything on day one. Work through the list over your first week, and invite your team as soon as the basics are in place so they can learn the inbox while volumes are still low.

Tip: If you want the full reference for every setting mentioned here, keep the complete workspace administration guide open in another tab.

Day 1: Sign up and start your trial

Go to app.relaydesk.example and create your workspace with your work email address. Your free trial starts immediately and lasts 14 days. During the trial you get every Growth plan feature, including SMS, WhatsApp, Facebook and Instagram messages, Relay AI Copilot and unlimited SLA policies. No credit card is required.

The trial also includes 50 AI resolutions, so you can switch on Relay AI Autopilot and see how it handles real questions before you commit. An AI resolution is a conversation Autopilot closes with no human reply in the following 72 hours.

  1. Enter your name, work email and a password with at least 12 characters.
  2. Choose a workspace name and a short workspace handle, for example northwind. The handle is used in your email forwarding address, so pick something you're happy to keep.
  3. Set your workspace time zone. Business hours, SLA timers and reports all use it.
  4. Turn on two-factor authentication for your own account. See Set up two-factor authentication.

Day 1–2: Invite your team

Go to Settings → Team → Invite and add the people who will answer customers as Agents. Add people who only need to read tickets and leave internal notes, such as engineers, finance or warehouse staff, as light seats. Light seats are free on every plan; they can view tickets and add internal notes but can't reply to customers.

If you plan to stay on Starter after the trial, remember that Starter allows up to 10 agent seats and 50 light seats. Growth and Scale have no agent seat limit. Full details are in Invite teammates.

Day 2: Connect your support email

Every workspace gets a forwarding address in the form support-<workspace>@inbound.relaydesk.example. Forward your existing support mailbox, for example support@northwind.example, to that address and new emails become tickets in the Shared Inbox.

  1. Go to Settings → Channels → Email and copy your forwarding address.
  2. Set up forwarding in Gmail, Google Workspace or Microsoft 365.
  3. Add SPF and DKIM records for your domain so replies sent from Relaydesk arrive from your own address and don't land in spam.

Step-by-step instructions for each provider are in Connect your support email.

Day 3: Install the chat widget

Copy the snippet from Settings → Channels → Live Chat and paste it before the closing </body> tag on your website. The widget is about 38 KB gzipped and loads asynchronously, so it won't slow your pages down. While you're there, choose a pre-chat form and decide what happens when nobody is online: in offline mode, chats become email tickets automatically. See Install the chat widget.

Day 3–4: Set up routing

Before tickets start piling up, decide who gets what. Two settings matter most in the first week:

  • Business hours. Add at least one schedule so SLA timers pause overnight and on weekends. Starter includes one schedule, Growth up to five and Scale unlimited.
  • Assignment rules. Start with round-robin. You can move to load-balanced or skills-based assignment once you know your volumes. See Assignment rules.

If you've promised customers a response time, create an SLA policy now too. Read SLA policies for how first reply, next reply and full resolution targets work.

Day 4: Create your first macros

Macros are saved replies that can also set tags, change status or assign a ticket in one click. Look at the last 50 emails your team answered and write a macro for every question that appeared more than twice. Most teams start with five to ten: order status, refund request, password help, shipping times and a polite "we're looking into it" holding reply. See Create and use macros.

Day 5: Publish your Knowledge Base

Turn your best macros into public articles. Go to Knowledge Base → New article, write the answer, add a category and click Publish. Even five good articles make a difference: customers find answers on their own, and Relay AI Autopilot uses your published articles as the source for every answer it gives.

When you're ready, connect a custom domain such as help.northwind.example. Learn more on the Knowledge Base product page.

Day 6–7: Try Relay AI and review

With articles published, open Settings → Relay AI and switch on Copilot for your agents. It drafts replies and summarizes long threads. Then turn on Autopilot for chat to start using your 50 trial resolutions. Autopilot hands off to a human whenever its confidence is low or the customer asks for a person. Setup details are in Set up Relay AI.

At the end of the week, open Reports and check first reply time and ticket volume by channel. These numbers tell you whether to adjust assignment, add macros or write more articles.

Before your trial ends

Pick a plan from Settings → Billing before day 14. Starter costs $19 per agent per month ($15 on annual billing), Growth $49 ($39 annual) and Scale $89 ($71 annual). Growth keeps everything you used during the trial. Compare plans on the pricing page.
